## Introduction

This worksheet introduces the fundamental concepts of exponents and logarithms. We will explore positive, negative, and fractional exponents, and learn how logarithms relate to exponents. By the end, you'll understand how these concepts interact with fractions and each other, and practice using these operations in various contexts.

---

## Part A: Understanding Exponents (12 problems)

Simplify or evaluate the following. Show your work.

**1.** $2^3$

**2.** $5^{-2}$

**3.** $9^{1/2}$ *(Hint: Think of square roots)*

**4.** $16^{3/4}$ *(Hint: A power and a root)*

**5.** Simplify: $(3^2)^4$

**6.** Simplify: $(x^3)^2 \cdot x^{-4}$

**7.** Evaluate: $\frac{4^{-2}}{2^3}$ *(Hint: Negative exponents flip the fraction)*

**8.** Simplify: $27^{-2/3}$ *(Hint: Start with the cube root)*

## Part B: Basics of Logarithms (10 problems)

Recall: A logarithm answers the question, "+To what power must a base be raised, to produce a given number?"+ For example, $\log_2(8) = 3$ because $2^3 = 8$.

Simplify or solve for $x$:

**13.** $\log_{10}(1000)$

**14.** $\log_2(32)$

**15.** $\log_5(\frac{1}{125})$

**16.** $\log_7(\frac{49}{7})$

**17.** If $\log_b(81) = 4$, what is $b$?

**18.** Write $9 = 3^2$ in logarithmic form.

**19.** Write $\log_5(\frac{1}{25})$ as a fraction.

**20.** Simplify: $\log_3(27) \cdot \log_3(9)$

**21.** Solve for $x$: $\log_4(x) = 3$

**22.** True or False: $\log(10^x) = x$. Explain why.

## Part C: Exponents and Logarithms Together (8 problems)

These problems explore how exponents and logarithms interact.

**23.** Simplify: $\log_2(2^5)$

**24.** Rewrite as a single logarithm: $\log_3(5) + \log_3(4)$

**25.** Rewrite as a single logarithm: $\log_2(8) - \log_2(4)$

**26.** Simplify: $3^{\log_3(81)}$

**27.** Solve for $x$: $\log_2(x) + \log_2(4) = 5$

**28.** If $\log_a(b) = 2$, what is $b$ in terms of $a$?

**29.** Simplify: $\frac{\log_5(125)}{\log_5(25)}$

**30.** Solve for $x$: $2^{2x-1} = 32$
